My App Store conversion went from 1% to 5.5%.

The first version of my app had no nice mascots or anything. I was focused only on UX, and the numbers were poor.

So I emailed every person using the app. To the ones who cancelled, I asked why, and said I'd try to make the app better.

All the replies started with: I just don't like it.

After 3 or 4 emails, I realized bad UI was the problem. My feeling: when they're not explaining a broken flow or complexity, the visual is the main blocker.

So I made it an actual app: very nice in both UI and UX.

Users hate AI slop, so you can't get a nice UI by asking AI to design it. You have to think at every step. Use it yourself and see how your UI works with your UX.

Example: you can't use a car speedometer to set the price adjustment you add to 3D models. Instead, I made a slider that is filled 20% by default. That 20% is the base cost, the starting point. You can't go left. You can only go right, and that shows how much markup you add.

Retention got much higher.

Right now people don't just look for a solution to their problem. They look for a simple, fast and beautiful one. You need both.
